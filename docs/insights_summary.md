**Period Analysed:** October 2016 – September 2018  
**Dataset:** ~99,000 orders, ~32,000 products, ~99,000 customers  
**Total GMV:** ~R$ 13.6M BRL

## 2. Revenue Trends

### 2.1 Strong Growth Trajectory

Revenue grew consistently from Q4 2016 through mid-2018, with particularly strong acceleration in Q1–Q2 2018. Month-on-month growth averaged **+8.3%** across the dataset period.

**Key observation:** November 2017 shows a pronounced spike (approximately 2.4× the October baseline), consistent with Brazil's Black Friday activity. This represents the single largest revenue month in the dataset.

### 2.2 Seasonality Patterns

- **November** (Black Friday): Strongest month; ~2–3× normal order volume
- **Q1 (Jan–Mar)**: Post-holiday dip followed by steady recovery
- **Mid-year (May–Jul)**: Stable baseline; slight softening
- **Q3 (Aug–Sep)**: Early signs of pre-Christmas demand ramp-up

**Recommendation:** Invest in inventory preparation and logistics capacity for November campaigns. Build marketing budgets that capture the pre-Black Friday demand window (October).

## 3. Product Performance Insights

### 3.1 Top Revenue Categories

| Rank | Category | Est. Revenue Share |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Health & Beauty | ~11% |
| 2 | Watches & Gifts | ~10% |
| 3 | Bed, Bath & Table | ~9% |
| 4 | Sports & Leisure | ~8% |
| 5 | Computers & Accessories | ~7% |

### 3.2 High-Volume / Low-Value Categories (Volume Drivers)

Categories with high order counts but modest per-order revenue (e.g. **Stationery**, **Garden Tools**) represent strong acquisition channels: customers enter through low-risk purchases, making these ideal for upsell/cross-sell programmes.

### 3.3 Low-Performance Categories

Categories such as **Fashion Children's Clothes**, **CDs / DVDs / Musicals**, and **Security & Services** generate minimal revenue and should be evaluated for catalogue relevance.

**Recommendation:** Prioritise merchandising and promotional spend on the top 5 revenue categories. Consider sunsetting or consolidating the bottom 10 categories, which collectively account for < 2% of GMV.

### 4.1 Geographic Revenue Concentration

| State | Revenue Share | Notes |

| SP (São Paulo) | ~42% | Dominant; large urban population |
| RJ (Rio de Janeiro) | ~13% | Second market |
| MG (Minas Gerais) | ~12% | Strong growth trajectory |
| RS, PR | ~5–6% each | Southern market |
| All others | ~22% | Significant untapped potential |

### 4.2 Geographic Opportunities

- The **North and Northeast regions** (AM, PA, BA, CE, MA, PI) are currently under-represented relative to population size.
- Average order values in northern states are comparable to southeastern states, suggesting demand exists but logistics friction suppresses conversion.

**Recommendation:** Partner with regional carriers to reduce freight costs in northern states. A 20% freight reduction could unlock an estimated 15–25% order volume increase in under-served regions, based on elasticity assumptions.

## 5. Delivery Performance Insights

### 5.1 Overall Performance

- **On-time delivery rate:** ~92.3%
- **Average delivery duration:** ~12.1 days
- The benchmark for competitive Brazilian e-commerce is 7–10 days for southeastern deliveries.

### 5.2 Late Delivery Patterns

- Late deliveries are disproportionately concentrated in:
  - **Remote northern states** (AM, RR, AP): average 22+ days delivery
  - **High-demand periods** (November, Q4): late rate rises to ~14%
  - **Specific product categories** with heavy/oversized items

### 5.3 Delivery Duration Trend

Delivery performance improved over the dataset period: average delivery duration fell from ~15 days in Q4 2016 to ~10 days by mid-2018, indicating logistics maturation.

**Recommendation:** Establish a carrier SLA dashboard monitoring on-time rates by state and carrier. Introduce customer compensation (credits/coupons) for deliveries exceeding SLA by > 5 days to protect NPS scores.

---

## 6. Customer Behaviour Insights

### 6.1 Repeat Purchase Rate

Only ~3% of customers place a second order within the dataset period. This is a critical retention challenge: the majority of Olist's revenue depends on acquiring new customers.

### 6.2 Customer Lifetime Value

Given the low repeat purchase rate, **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)** efficiency is paramount. The business model depends heavily on the seller ecosystem driving organic discovery.

### 6.3 Payment Preferences

- Credit card (installments) is the dominant payment method
- Average installment count: ~3 months
- Orders paid in full upfront have slightly lower AOV, suggesting installments enable larger basket sizes

**Recommendation:** Introduce a structured post-purchase email retention programme targeting customers within 30 and 90 days of their first purchase. Personalise recommendations based on initial category purchase. A 2–3% improvement in 90-day repeat rate would add an estimated R$ 400K–600K to annual GMV.

## 8. Data Limitations & Caveats

1. **Dataset period is 2016–2018.** Brazilian e-commerce and logistics have evolved significantly since then.
2. **No marketing/acquisition data.** CAC and channel attribution analysis are not possible with this dataset.
3. **Seller data is anonymised.** Seller-level performance insights are limited.
4. **No returns/refund data.** Net revenue figures may be slightly overstated.
5. **Review data not incorporated** into this analysis but represents a rich source for NPS and product quality insights.
